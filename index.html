Model assumptions & teaching connections

This is an illustrative weekly model, not a forecast or the original simulator's recovered code. This web version calculates locally in your browser using the same model as the companion Python project. Amounts are AUD, excluding GST and income tax. Loan balance is $20,000; only interest is counted. Demand is an expected whole-number estimate, without random events. Capacity is 900 cups, or 1,150 with technology. Unsold ingredients expire after one week.

Base demand: 800 cups at $7 before adjustments. Higher prices reduce demand; advertising has diminishing returns; confidence changes spending. Low-sugar products respond to health trends; sustainable packaging adds cost and an assumed 8% demand benefit. Technology adds a $120 weekly lease/support cost and reduces labour cost by 12%. All coefficients are chosen for classroom demonstration, not derived from empirical evidence.

Demand = 800 × exp[−0.20(price−7)] × confidence/100 × [1+0.10 ln(1+advertising/100)] × health factor × packaging factor. Health factor = 1+0.25h with a low-sugar menu, otherwise 1−0.30h; h is the trend score divided by 100.

Teaching links from the supplied resources: KK1 internal, operating and macro environments; KK3 societal values and trends; KK4 confidence, inflation and interest costs; KK5 technology in business operations. The supplied 7 May lesson plan uses a bubble tea shop. Employment, tax and legal regulation are discussion extensions, not calculated here.

Predict → Change one variable → Compare → Justify. All changes are measured against the fixed normal-trading baseline.
